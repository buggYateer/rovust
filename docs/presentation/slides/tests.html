How we made sure the numbers mean something

58

unit tests, 8 stages

Motor curve non-linear · encoder direction · P keeps a steady-state error, PI/PID remove it · straight driving · each sensor’s physics and faults · fusion beats odometry · map precision · A* · missions in every world · subsumption · teleop, watchdog, link loss · alert timing · game-controller driving · the Qt window in English and Arabic.

264

headless runs

Same mission object, no window, 7 processes in parallel. Every seed = a different generated map and noise sequence; results are mean ± standard deviation. Identical conditions for every strategy and sensor configuration — the fairness the proposal promised.

6

bugs found by the physics, not by us

Walls erased by lost echoes · walls thickened by hits placed on the surface · a 1 m ring of phantom obstacles from IR noise at long range · a rover pinned to a reef by the current · rovers lost to pits behind and beside them · a map rotated by gyro bias. Each became a test.

Control: faulting a sensor that is not mounted in a world (the sun sensor in the cave, the DVL on land) reproduces the baseline exactly in every run — the experiment harness changes only what it claims to change.

Rovust · Results · tests/, sim/experiments/run_batch.py

A word on method. Fifty-eight tests pin the physics, the behaviours, the alert timing and the game controller, so that a change in one layer cannot silently break another. The experiments run the identical mission object without a window, many seeds in parallel, and report means with their spread. And several of our hardest bugs were found by the simulated physics itself — the specular echoes erasing walls, for example — and each one turned into a test.

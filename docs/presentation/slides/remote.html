Console — a web page served by the simulator over Wi-Fi: HUD over the map, joystick in a ring of live sonar readings (amber and red where something is close), big STOP, Lights, Home, speed −/+, Arabic or English.

Game controller — USB or Bluetooth (SDL2 on the desktop, Gamepad API on the phone). ✕ gas, △ reverse, stick steers; the stick alone points where to go; Start stops, ○ lights, □ home, L1/R1 mode.

Auto / Assisted / Manual — in assisted the safety layers still override you; in manual nothing does. Phone, controller and keyboard all send over the same radio.

Radio link model — signal = f(distance, walls on the line of sight); weak: half the packets lost; lost for 3 s: return home, explore alone or wait. Mars adds a 2 s delay, the underwater modem 0.4 s.

One calm alert — stuck, weak link, low battery, gas, failed sensor, too dark… one shared rule set: shown after 0.8 s, held ≥ 3 s, worse news takes over after 1 s. A chip, never a popup.

Rovust · Implementation · comms.py, teleop/, alerts.py, qtui/gamepad.py — the “communication” activity of mobile robots

Remote control is where the communication activity from Robot Behaviour meets the argument for autonomy. The desktop opens a console for a phone or tablet and shows its address and a QR code: scan it and you drive, with the joystick inside a ring of live sonar readings. A game controller works too, on the desktop or on the phone, and it drives like a racing game: cross is gas, triangle is reverse, the stick steers, and the stick alone means "go that way". Whatever you drive with, every command travels through a simulated radio link that weakens with distance and with each wall between the rover and its base. When you drive deep into the cave the link drops, a short alert says so — the same alert rules on the desktop and the phone, held long enough to read, never a popup — and after three seconds the rover takes over and drives itself back into range. The lesson writes itself: a rover that cannot decide alone is lost the moment the radio is.

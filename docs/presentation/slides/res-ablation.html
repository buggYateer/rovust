Sonar is the mapping sensor: without it the rover survives on IR and bumpers but cannot see far enough to map.

Ground speed is the localisation sensor: wheel slip is that large.

An absolute heading is indispensable: the gyro’s bias turns the whole map.

IR and bumpers are redundant with the sonar here — the last line of defence, not the first.

Faulting a sensor that is not mounted reproduces the baseline exactly — the harness is sound.

Rovust · Results · DFS strategy, 4 seeds per case, 300 s; “—” = sensor not mounted in that world

The ablation answers the question the supervisor cares about most: which sensors earn their place. Kill the sonar and coverage collapses; kill the ground-speed sensor and the estimate is five to sixteen metres off; kill the compass or sun sensor and the map rotates away. Kill the cliff switches and one rover in four ends in a crater. The infrared and bumpers cost almost nothing to lose — they are the last line of defence, which is exactly the role we gave them.
